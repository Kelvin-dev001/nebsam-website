# BAOFENG UV-5R SHORT-RANGE RADIO

## DUAL-BAND, 128 CHANNELS, SELECTABLE 5W OR 1W

**A dual-band handheld with 128 channels, switchable 5W high or 1W low transmit power, dual standby display, built-in VOX and a documented operating range of 5-10km on open ground.**

The Baofeng UV-5R is a conventional short-range two-way radio. It transmits directly to other radios with no network, SIM card or data connection involved, and offers more channel capacity and more configurability than the entry-level models in the range.

## Items requiring verification before publication — READ ITEM 1 FIRST

1. **`[[NEEDS_VERIFICATION: Baofeng UV-5R transmit and receive frequency range]]` — this is an accuracy trap and the single most important item on this page.** The source lists "FM Radio Frequency range: 65-108MHz". That is the **broadcast FM receiver** band — the feature that lets the user listen to commercial radio stations. It is **not** the transceiver's transmit and receive range, which the source never states. The UV-5R's actual two-way band is widely known from other sources, but it is not in this document and has deliberately not been written into this page. Obtain it from the manufacturer datasheet. Do not let anyone "correct" the omission from memory: publishing an unverified frequency range on a radio product page is both a specification error and, on licensed spectrum, a compliance one.
2. `[[NEEDS_VERIFICATION: CAK licensing for short-range frequency radios]]` — applies to all five short-range models. See the BF-888s notes.
3. `[[NEEDS_VERIFICATION: Baofeng UV-5R price]]` — no price in any supplied document.
4. `[[NEEDS_VERIFICATION: Baofeng UV-5R battery capacity, standby and talk time, IP rating, speaker, LCD size]]` — undocumented.

## Claims requiring care

The stated range of "Open ground: 5-10km, Obstacle ground: 3-5km" is the source's own figure and is reproduced as such. Present it as typical performance, dependent on terrain, obstructions, antenna and power setting — not as a guarantee. Note also that the broader radio-communication material carries the phrase "unlimited distances without interference"; that claim is unpublishable (brief section 4.2) and is doubly wrong for a short-range radio, which has a hard physical range limit.
